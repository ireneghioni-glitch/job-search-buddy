**Introduction**
----------------

We seek a dedicated and highly motivated PhD student to join the Architecture and Climate group at the Louvain research institute for Landscape, Architecture, Built environment (LAB), UCLouvain, based at the Brussels Saint-Gilles campus. We study multi-energy flows in and around buildings and districts through integrated passive and active systems. Our work ranges from prototyping experimental cyber-physical building systems with a focus on occupant well-being, through the development of coupled simulation, optimization and artificial intelligence models, to participatory digital tools that put this work in the hands of planners and decision makers. Our objective is to advance the renewable energy transition, as well as material and energy circularity, by tackling the gaps and barriers that currently stand in the way of a future climate-proof, sustainable built environment.

**The project**  
  
Buildings are evolving from passive energy consumers into grid-interactive “prosumers” that generate, store, share and flexibly shift energy. Their uptake, however, is held back by technical, economic and social barriers, and by a lack of decision support tools that give non-experts, such as building owners and occupants, access to expertise from engineering (grid operation and multi-energy systems) and economics (multi-period financial optimization) to make informed decisions. This PhD will develop and test an AI-assisted recommender system for the participatory design of smart, energy-flexible buildings and energy communities. It will combine physics-informed machine learning surrogate models for building performance with energy system planning and operation models (demand response, local energy sharing, dynamic tariffs and grid emissions), and Large Language Models that provide reliable design guidance by retrieving validated knowledge (Retrieval-Augmented Generation, RAG) and by directly calling the simulation and optimization models as tools (agentic workflows, e.g. via the Model Context Protocol, MCP). The framework will be applied to a Belgian energy community case study and evaluated in co-creation workshops with real stakeholders.

The objective is to understand real-world behavioral barriers relevant to sustainable building renovation and smart energy technologies, and to develop a decision support framework that addresses these barriers by empowering the stakeholders most affected by them: occupants and homeowners.
