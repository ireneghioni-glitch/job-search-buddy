**YOUR TASKS**

  

The project is a translational research initiative bringing together clinical, scientific and valorisation expertise to develop an innovative solution for advanced sinus surgery.

The project is led by Prof. Thibaut Van Zele (Department of Otorhinolaryngology, Ghent University Hospital), Prof. Peter Dubruel (Polymer Chemistry & Biomaterials Group, Ghent University). The interdisciplinary team combines internationally recognised expertise in sinus surgery, biomaterials and polymer technologies, complemented by dedicated expertise in business development and technology valorisation. This collaborative environment supports the translation of scientific innovations into clinically relevant and commercially viable medical technologies.

You will contribute to the further development and preclinical validation of an innovative biomaterial-based medical device, with a focus on translating existing research outcomes towards clinical application. Working within an interdisciplinary team of polymer scientists, engineers and clinicians, you will focus on formulation optimisation, material characterisation and experimental validation.

Your main responsibilities will include:

* Develop and optimise polymer formulations using existing, ready-to-use building blocks, with a focus on reproducibility, performance and processability.
* Characterise the physicochemical properties of the formulations, including rheological behaviour, solidification kinetics and mechanical performance.
* Incorporate active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) into the formulations and investigate their distribution and release profiles under physiologically relevant conditions.
* Investigate the impact of different sterilisation methods on material properties and performance.
* Collaborate with external engineering partners to evaluate and optimise prototype designs.
* Conduct biocompatibility assessments in accordance with relevant ISO 10993 standards.
* Contribute to the development of experimental protocols, technical documentation and planning of upcoming in vivo validation studies.
