**About The Role**
------------------

Buyers of business software increasingly need to know how well AI agents actually perform, not just what vendors claim. G2 is building independent, rigorous evaluations of AI agents on realistic business work, to help buyers choose with confidence and to set a trusted standard for the market.

This is the most senior applied research role on that work, and it is hands-on. You will own the research agenda for how agents get tested, make sure the results mean what we say they mean, and turn them into product features and insights G2 publishes. You will work side by side with engineers and product managers, and your work will go into what G2 ships today, not only into longer-term research.

You will also raise the bar around you. You will mentor engineers, data scientists and researchers working on evaluation, review their methodology, and make the standards you set repeatable by people who did not write them.

The field is unsettled. Methods from academia, frontier labs and industry are still being worked out. Part of the job is staying on top of that work and bringing the ideas that matter into the product. The approaches that last are the simple, general ones that still hold up as models change.

**In This Role, You Will:**
---------------------------

* **Own the evaluation methodology.** Set the standard for how G2 evaluates AI agents, defend it to skeptical audiences inside and outside the company, and keep it current as models change.
* **Contribute to today's product.** Work closely with engineers and product managers to build and improve the evaluations behind G2's current offerings. Help scope features, turn methods into production-ready components, and iterate on what customers and the business need.
* **Design realistic evaluations.** Build environments and task sets that reflect the work businesses actually give AI agents, and that stay useful as models improve.
* **Develop sound measurement.** Create scoring approaches, including automated checks, LLM-based judges and human review, that are accurate, reproducible and hard to game. Measure consistency and reliability, not just average performance.
* **Validate the evaluations themselves.** Audit tasks and graders, quantify noise, and establish when differences between agents are real.
* **Represent the results.** Present and defend evaluation findings to buyers, customers and partners, and to the research community. Be the credible voice behind the numbers when the audience has a stake in the outcome.
* **Mentor and multiply.** Manage and guide researchers and engineers on evaluation design and measurement, review their work, and write the documentation and standards that let the team run evaluations without you in the room.
* **Set and pursue a research agenda.** Choose which evaluation problems matter most and carry long-running projects through to something that works, favoring simple ideas that generalize over ones tuned to this year's models. Directions include simulated-user and environment-based evaluation in the tradition of benchmarks like τ-bench; realistic user and persona simulation; LLM-as-judge reliability; evaluation of voice and multimodal agents; assessing agents in open-ended, long-horizon workflows; and protecting test sets from contamination.
* **Stay current.** Follow new benchmarks, papers and evaluation practices across the research community. Translate them into product and roadmap decisions, and communicate them clearly to the team.
* **Share and collaborate.** Write methodology documentation and, where appropriate, papers and public posts. Partner with data and go-to-market teams on how results are presented and used.
