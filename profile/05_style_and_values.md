# Style, values, and preferences — Irene Ghioni

## How I write
- **Tone:** semi-formal, direct, professional but personal. I use "I" (first person) in cover letters.
- **Sentences:** generally short and direct. Clear structure: context → problem → what I did → result.
- **I open with concrete context**, not with generic phrases. Example: "I spent over two years as a Formulation Chemist developing UV-curable inks..."
- **I use concrete numbers** whenever possible (R²=0.986, -40/50%, 3+ years).
- **I honestly admit limitations**. Example: "I can't speak Dutch fluently yet, but I'm highly motivated to learn it." — I don't hide weaknesses, I contextualize them.
- **I connect past and future in a narrative**: "I know the bottleneck from the lab side, and now I'm building the bridge to the data side."
- **I avoid:** empty corporate language ("dynamic professional", "results-oriented", "passionate about"), superlatives not supported by facts, marketing jargon.
- **I use bold** to highlight key points in cover letters and posts.

### Example of my style (excerpt from LinkedIn post)
> "From industrial chemistry to data science: I'm building a model that predicts UV-curing conversion directly from molecular structure. I spent over two years as a Formulation Chemist developing UV-curable and water-based inks, and one year as an Analytical Researcher supporting the Photoinitiators Synthesis Lab with advanced analysis. I know how much time and how many trials it takes to optimize a formulation. I know the bottleneck from the lab side, and now I'm building the bridge to the data side."

### Example of my style (excerpt from cover letter)
> "It was during my experience as a formulator that, with tens of tested formulations and a lot of lab data acquired, I got interested in the possibility of delivering new insights and valuable results using all that data: could it have been possible to optimize the decision and prioritization step of my daily work so that I could focus more on the right formulations?"

## Professional values
- **Concrete scientific impact**: I want to build tools and models that solve real R&D problems, not theoretical exercises.
- **Continuous learning**: I'm investing in a career transition because I believe in the value of integrating science + data.
- **Intellectual honesty**: I prefer to say "I don't know yet" than to fake competence.
- **Cross-disciplinarity**: my value lies in uniting two worlds (chemistry and data), not in becoming an expert in just one.
- **Collaboration**: I've worked in structured R&D teams and I know how to navigate complex environments (synthesis, analytical, suppliers, patents).

---

## What I do NOT want
- Purely IT or software consulting roles with no contact with the scientific domain
- Companies that don't invest in digitalization / data-driven R&D (I would be underutilized)
- Sales or purely commercial roles
- Environments where seniority is only tenure and not competence

---

## Deal-breakers
- No unpaid internships (aside from the mandatory BeCode internship already in progress)
- Very frequent travel (>30%)
- Roles requiring me to completely abandon my chemical/scientific background
